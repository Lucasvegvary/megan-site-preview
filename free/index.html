What's Inside

The 7 signs — and one gentle thing to try today

You don't have to figure out what every sign means. You only have to notice. Here's the heart of the guide: each sign, with one small, safe practice you can do today. Take what resonates, and leave the rest.

Sign One

The repeating number or song

The same number, the same lyric, again and again. Try today: Each time it shows up, jot down where you were and what you were thinking. Look for a pattern at week's end — no meaning required, just notice.

Sign Two

The gut "no" you talked yourself out of

A quiet refusal you overrode with logic. Try today: Recall one recent "no" you ignored. Write one sentence on how your body felt in that moment — tight, heavy, light? Let the body be a source of information.

Sign Three

The dream that lingered

A dream that stayed with you all day. Try today: Write down what you remember and one feeling it left behind. You're not interpreting — you're keeping a record so the threads become visible over time.

Sign Four

The name that keeps surfacing

A name that keeps appearing in conversation, media, memory. Try today: Notice your honest reaction when it surfaces — warmth, tension, curiosity? Naming the feeling is enough for now.

Sign Five

The sudden heaviness around a certain person

A weight you feel near someone specific. Try today: Note when it arrives and when it lifts. You're simply gathering observations about your own energy — not judging anyone, just listening to yourself.

Sign Six

The goosebump "yes"

That full-body chill when something rings true. Try today: When it happens, pause and finish the sentence: "That felt true because…" Let your own knowing speak first, before your thinking mind catches up.

Sign Seven

The quiet pull toward change

A steady, unglamorous tug toward something different. Try today: Write down the smallest honest next step the pull is asking for — one you could take this week. You stay in the lead; the pull just points. Small is allowed.

A gentle reminder: this guide is for reflection and personal insight, not diagnosis, prediction, or medical advice. Trust what's yours to trust, and set the rest down.
