How to Prepare for Your First Intuitive Reading

By Megan · Intuitive, Healer & Guide

To prepare for your first intuitive reading, set one simple intention, jot down a few open questions you'd like to explore, and come as you are — no special belief or ritual required. Then stay open and curious, take notes, and keep what resonates while leaving the rest. That's truly all it takes.

If you're booking your first intuitive reading and feeling a little unsure — maybe curious, maybe a touch skeptical — you're in exactly the right place. There's no test to pass and nothing to perform. The most useful preparation for a psychic reading is also the simplest: arrive grounded, bring your honest questions, and stay open. Set the heavy thing down for an hour, and pick your next step up.

What a reading is — and isn't

A grounded intuitive reading is a calm, focused conversation in which Megan tunes in to the energy around your situation and reflects back what she senses — patterns, possibilities, and the deeper currents under your questions. It's insight and perspective, offered with care. It is not fortune-telling, fear, or a fixed verdict about your future. Nothing is set in stone, and you always keep your free will and your choices.

That distinction matters most if part of you is skeptical. You don't have to switch off your discernment to get value from a reading — in fact, your discernment is welcome. A trustworthy reading should leave you with something usable: a clearer next step, a softer relationship with a hard situation, or a question worth sitting with. If it ever trades in fear or pressure, that's your cue to step back. Grounded guidance never leans on fear.

Step 1 — Set a simple intention

Before your session, take a quiet minute to ask yourself: what do I most want clarity on right now? Maybe it's a decision you keep circling. Maybe it's a relationship, a transition, or simply where to put your energy next. You don't need a polished answer — one honest intention is plenty. Naming it helps focus the reading the way pointing a flashlight focuses its beam.

If nothing specific surfaces, that's completely fine too. "I'd like clarity and a sense of direction" is a perfectly good intention. The point isn't to script the session — it's to arrive knowing roughly what matters to you.

Step 2 — Jot down what to ask

One of the most common first-reading worries is what to ask. Here's the gentle secret: open questions invite far more guidance than yes-or-no ones. Instead of "Will I get the job?", try "What's the deeper thing I'm really weighing here?" or "How can I move through this transition with more ease?" Open questions give the reading room to breathe.

Write down two or three that genuinely matter to you. A few that tend to open good doors:

  • What's the real question underneath the one I think I'm asking?
  • What pattern keeps showing up in this part of my life — and why?
  • What's ready to be released, and what's ready to grow?
  • How can I best support myself through what's ahead?
  • Where is my energy actually wanting to go right now?

Keep your list close, but hold it loosely. Often the most meaningful insight arrives somewhere you didn't think to look.

Step 3 — Come as you are

You don't need to meditate for an hour, light anything, believe a certain amount, or "get into the right headspace." You're allowed to arrive tired, uncertain, or skeptical. All of that is welcome — you're met exactly where you are.

A little practical comfort does help the experience, though. Find a quiet spot where you won't be interrupted. Have water nearby. If your reading is online, settle somewhere with a stable connection and good privacy so you can speak freely — intuition isn't limited by distance, so an online reading is every bit as real as an in-person one. Then take one slow breath and let your shoulders drop. That's the whole ritual.

Step 4 — Stay open and curious

During the reading, let it unfold rather than forcing it. Some messages will land instantly with a quiet click of recognition. Others may not make sense until later — sometimes days later. Both are normal. You don't have to agree with everything or make it all fit on the spot.

This is where you stay in the lead: take what resonates and gently set aside what doesn't. A grounded reading is a collaboration, not a pronouncement. Your inner "yes" and your honest "hmm, not sure" are both useful information, and you're trusted to use them.

Step 5 — Take notes

Keep a notebook or your phone within reach and jot down the words, images, and feelings that stand out — even fragments. In the moment it's easy to think you'll remember everything; afterward, a single noted phrase can become the thread you keep pulling for weeks. Insights from a reading tend to ripen with time, and your notes let you return to them as life catches up to the guidance.

A note for the skeptics

If you're approaching this with a healthy dose of "we'll see," you're not the wrong kind of client — you might be the ideal one. The most grounding thing you can do is stay curious and notice what's actually useful. You don't have to believe anything in advance. Bring your real questions, watch what resonates, and let the value speak for itself. No theatrics, no pressure, no fear — just a thoughtful conversation that leaves you with a usable takeaway.
